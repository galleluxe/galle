## In Search of the Perfect Rose

The rose harvest in Grasse begins at dawn. By 9 am, the fragile petals begin to wilt, their volatile aromatic compounds escaping rapidly into the warming Provençal air. The window of harvest is narrow: two hours at most.

We travelled to Grasse in early May — peak season — to meet our supplier, a third-generation floriculturist whose family has cultivated Rosa centifolia on the same hillside since 1948.

### Rosa Centifolia vs. Rosa Damascena

There are two queens of the perfume rose world.

**Rosa damascena** — the Damask rose — is the workhorse of Bulgarian and Turkish perfumery. Its scent is rich, honeyed, and direct: what most people think of when they imagine "rose perfume."

**Rosa centifolia** — the Cabbage rose, or *rose de mai* — is more elusive. Its scent is powdery, intensely honeyed, with a slight green and violet undertone that gives it a quality perfumers describe as *ineffable*. It is what we use in **Entice**.

### The Extraction Process

Traditional enfleurage — pressing petals into animal fat — has been largely replaced by solvent extraction, producing a "concrete" that is further processed into an "absolute." The absolute of Rosa centifolia is one of the most expensive raw materials in perfumery, priced by the kilogram at a premium that rivals precious metals.

We believe the expense is non-negotiable. There is no synthetic substitute that captures the true rose.

### What Arrives in Your Bottle

Each 50ml bottle of Entice contains material derived from approximately 14 kilograms of handpicked Rosa centifolia petals — harvested, extracted, and aged before they become part of your story.
